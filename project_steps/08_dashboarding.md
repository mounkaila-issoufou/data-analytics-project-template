# Step 08 — Data Visualization & Dashboards

## Purpose of this step
This step focuses on transforming business insights
into **clear, accurate, and actionable visualizations**.

The objective is not to display all available data,
but to **support decision-making** through well-designed dashboards.

---

## Role of dashboards in a data project

Dashboards are:
- A communication tool
- A decision support interface
- A monitoring mechanism

They are NOT:
- Data dumps
- Exploratory analysis tools
- Replacements for business thinking

---

## Inputs
- Validated KPIs
- Business insights from Step 07
- Analytics SQL queries

Dashboards must only rely on **trusted analytical data**.

---

## Dashboard design principles

A good dashboard:
- Answers specific business questions
- Has a clear audience
- Prioritizes clarity over aesthetics
- Guides the reader’s attention

Avoid overcrowding and unnecessary complexity.

---

## Structure of a dashboard

Recommended layout:
1. High-level KPIs (overview)
2. Trends over time
3. Breakdown by key dimensions
4. Drill-down or diagnostic views

Each section should have a clear purpose.

---

## KPI selection
Only include KPIs that:
- Are business-relevant
- Are actionable
- Have a clear owner

If a KPI does not drive a decision, it should not be displayed.

---

## Visualization best practices

- Choose the right chart for the message
- Use consistent scales and colors
- Avoid misleading axes
- Limit the number of colors
- Label clearly

Charts should explain themselves.

---

## Interactivity
Use filters and drill-downs sparingly:
- Time period
- Product
- Region
- Customer segment

Interactivity should enhance analysis, not confuse users.

---

## Tools
Dashboards may be built using:
- Power BI
- Looker
- Tableau
- Other BI tools

Tool choice is secondary to design quality.

---

## Versioning and maintenance
Dashboards should:
- Be versioned
- Be documented
- Evolve with business needs

Any KPI change must be reflected consistently.

---

## Documentation
Each dashboard should be accompanied by:
- A short description
- KPI definitions
- Known limitations

---

## Output of this step
At the end of this step:
- Dashboards communicate insights clearly
- Stakeholders can monitor performance
- Decisions are supported by data
- The analytical work is visible and usable

This step ensures **insights are understood and acted upon**.
